5 · Assumptions

#AssumptionMeaning
A1Steady surfaceT(x,y) is the long-time equilibrium; time dependence is handled separately by ODEs for the hot-spot temperatures.
A2Thin boardNo variation across the thickness, so the board is a surface; thickness enters only through volumes (triple integrals).
A3Gaussian hot spotsChip i adds θi·exp(−|r−ci|²/2si²) with fixed spread si. The fan changes only the amplitude θi.
A4Newton coolingBoth faces lose heat at h(x,y)(T−Ta). Edges insulated, radiation neglected, board large against the hot spots (about 5% of the loss integral falls outside the board).
A5Gaussian fanh = hnat + Δh·exp(−|r−f|²/2σ²). A real fan is directional.
A6Averaged coolingThe cooling a hot spot feels is the profile-weighted average of h, a double integral done in polar coordinates.
A7Heat sharingHot spots exchange heat with conductance κij = k·t·wij/dij, a strip of width ½(si+sj) and length dij.
A8Chip temperatureChip temperature is the surface value at its centre; chip powers are fixed.
A9Illustrative numbersAll parameters are plausible but assumed. Conclusions are about the method and the shape of the answer, not a real board.

6 · Where each syllabus topic appears

Surfaces

T(x,y) and the fan profile h(x,y) are surfaces; the landscape in §2 is a surface over fan positions.

C⁰, C¹, C² features

Tmax has a corner (C⁰, not C¹) along the tie curve; the optimum sits on it. Gradients, level curves and critical points use C¹.

Line, double, triple integrals

h̄i is a double integral over the hot spot; stored heat is a triple integral; the energy balance sums the cooling integrals.

ODEs

Cθ′ = P − Mθ is a linear first-order system; §3 steps it with Euler and RK4, and its eigenvalues give the time constants.
