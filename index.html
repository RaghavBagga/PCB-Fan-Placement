Where should the fan go?

A printed circuit board with three heat-generating chips and one movable, localised fan. The temperature field is the solution of a steady heat equation. Calculus then finds the best fan position, explains why it is best, and checks the whole simulation against exact results.

The model in one line

Heat spreads sideways through the board and leaks into the air from both faces. In steady state the two balance at every point:

$$kt\,\nabla^2 T\;-\;2h(x,y)\,(T-T_a)\;+\;q(x,y)=0,\qquad h=h_{\rm nat}+\Delta h\,e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}$$

The fan is a Gaussian patch of extra convection centred at $(x_f,y_f)$. Moving it changes only the coefficient $h$, so the question is how the solution $T$ depends on $(x_f,y_f)$.

The main finding

The best position is not on the hottest chip. $T_{\max}$ is the maximum over several peaks, so at the optimum the CPU and regulator peaks are equal and their gradients point in opposite directions. That is a Lagrange (KKT) condition, and the analysis page verifies it numerically: the weighted gradient sum is zero to $10^{-3}$ K/mm.

Along the way the project uses every strand of the course: gradients and directional derivatives, second-derivative tests, Lagrange multipliers, polar double integrals, flux, circulation, the Divergence and Green theorems, curvature, Taylor series and an ODE with an exact solution.

What this is and is not. Everything on this site is computed from the stated model. The chip powers, conductivity, convection coefficients and the Gaussian fan shape are assumptions chosen for illustration, and nothing was validated against a physical board. The checks show that the equations are solved correctly, not that the model describes a real product.
