Theory

From physics to calculus

How the heat equation arises, how it becomes a linear system, and how derivatives of the solution with respect to the fan position are obtained exactly.

1. The heat equation and its length scale

For a thin board of thickness $t$ and effective in-plane conductivity $k$, the net heat flowing into a patch by conduction, $kt\nabla^2T$, balances loss through both faces, $2h(T-T_a)$, plus the generation $q$:

$$kt\,\nabla^2T-2h(x,y)(T-T_a)+q(x,y)=0,\qquad \nabla T\cdot\mathbf n=0\ \text{on the edges.}$$

Away from sources and with constant $h$ this becomes $\nabla^2\theta=\theta/\ell^2$ for $\theta=T-T_a$, with

$$\ell=\sqrt{\frac{kt}{2h}}=\sqrt{\frac{0.024}{20}}\ \text{m}\approx 34.6\ \text{mm}.$$

Heat from a source decays like $e^{-r/\ell}$, so a chip warms material within roughly $\ell$ of it. In one dimension the equation is the ODE $\theta''-\theta/\ell^2=-(q'/kt)\,\delta(x-x_0)$, whose exact solution (with insulated ends) is used as a test of the solver.

2. Discretisation and the Taylor-series error

On a square grid of spacing $\varepsilon$, adding the Taylor expansions of $u(x\pm\varepsilon,y)$ and $u(x,y\pm\varepsilon)$ gives

$$\frac{u_E+u_W+u_N+u_S-4u}{\varepsilon^2}=\nabla^2u+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{12}\bigl(u_{xxxx}+u_{yyyy}\bigr)+O(\varepsilon^4),$$

so the Laplacian is approximated with error $O(\varepsilon^2)$. Writing a heat balance for each square cell gives one equation per cell,

$$kt\sum_{\rm nbrs}(\theta_{\rm nb}-\theta_c)-2h_c\varepsilon^2\theta_c+q_c\varepsilon^2=0,$$

that is the linear system $A\boldsymbol\theta=\mathbf b$ with $A=A_c+2\varepsilon^2\,\mathrm{diag}(h)$. The conduction matrix $A_c$ is symmetric and independent of the fan, so $A$ is symmetric positive definite and only its diagonal changes when the fan moves. That is why the scan of 2091 positions takes about ten seconds in Python, and why the lab responds instantly. The solver is a preconditioned conjugate gradient method.

3. Conservation and the Divergence theorem

Integrating the equation over the board, the conduction term becomes a boundary integral by the Divergence theorem, and vanishes because the edges are insulated:

$$\sum_kP_k=\iint_\Omega 2h(T-T_a)\,dA.$$

Heat generated equals heat removed, for every fan position. The local form is $\operatorname{div}\mathbf F=q-2h\theta$ with flux $\mathbf F=-kt\nabla T$. The readout in the lab prints both sides of the balance on every update.

The fan's total added convection is a polar integral:

$$\iint\Delta h\,e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}dA=\Delta h\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}r\,dr\,d\phi=2\pi\sigma^2\Delta h.$$

This becomes the natural "airflow budget" constraint in the Lagrange problem.

4. The gradient with respect to the fan position, by an adjoint solve

Only $h$ depends on the fan position. Differentiating $A\boldsymbol\theta=\mathbf b$ with respect to $x_f$:

$$A\,\frac{\partial\boldsymbol\theta}{\partial x_f}=-\frac{\partial A}{\partial x_f}\boldsymbol\theta,\qquad \frac{\partial h}{\partial x_f}=\Delta h\,e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}\frac{x-x_f}{\sigma^2}.$$

For the temperature in one cell $i$, define $\boldsymbol\lambda=A^{-1}\mathbf e_i$ (one extra solve, using symmetry of $A$). Then

$$\frac{\partial T_i}{\partial x_f}=-\sum_m\lambda_m\,2\varepsilon^2\,\frac{\partial h_m}{\partial x_f}\,\theta_m,$$

and the same $\boldsymbol\lambda$ gives every other parameter derivative. One solve per chip peak yields the whole gradient, instead of two solves per parameter by finite differences. The result agrees with central differences to $1.6\times10^{-7}$ (relative, worst of 30 partial derivatives).

5. Optimality when peaks tie: KKT

$T_{\max}=\max_kf_k$ is smooth except where peaks tie. At a tie of two peaks, the directional derivative of $T_{\max}$ along $\mathbf d$ is $\max(\nabla f_1\!\cdot\mathbf d,\nabla f_2\!\cdot\mathbf d)$, so the point is a local minimum exactly when some weight $w\in[0,1]$ satisfies

$$w\,\nabla f_1+(1-w)\,\nabla f_2=\mathbf 0.$$

In the plane this means the two gradients are opposite. This is the Lagrange condition for "minimise $s$ subject to $f_1\le s,\ f_2\le s$", with $w$ as multiplier. Gradient descent uses the minimum-norm vector in the convex hull of the tied gradients.

6. Constrained placement problems

Rail. If the fan must lie on $y_f=c$, the condition $\nabla T_{\max}=\lambda\nabla g$ gives $\partial T_{\max}/\partial x_f=0$ along the rail and $\lambda=\partial T_{\max}/\partial y_f$. The multiplier is the shadow price of the rail: $dT^*_{\max}/dc=\lambda$.

Airflow budget. Hold $g=2\pi\sigma^2\Delta h-C=0$. With $F=\sum_kw_kf_k$ the weighted peak, the Lagrange condition reduces to an elasticity condition,

$$\sigma\,\frac{\partial F}{\partial\sigma}=2\Delta h\,\frac{\partial F}{\partial\Delta h},$$

meaning the next unit of budget is equally effective whether spent widening or strengthening the fan.
