Find the best position

The optimiser is a compass search on Tmax(xf, yf): it tries steps in eight directions, moves when Tmax falls and halves the step otherwise. It converges to a point where two chip peaks are equal, which is exactly the tie condition of the theory page. The dashed cyan path on the board is its trail.

Things to try

  • Park the fan on the CPU. The CPU gets cooler but the regulator becomes the hottest part. The yellow gradient arrow points toward the regulator side, showing why the best spot is a compromise.
  • Press Optimise. It stops near (37.7, 50.3) mm for the default fan, where the readout's hottest point jumps between the CPU and the regulator as you nudge the fan.
  • Switch the fan profile to the order-2 Taylor model. The paraboloid is the local Taylor model of the fan (each polynomial is used only where it is within 2% of the Gaussian, and set to zero beyond). It recovers well under half of the cooling because the real fan reaches far beyond that region. Higher orders recover more. Expect about 61 °C instead of 50 °C at the optimum.
  • Show heat-flow arrows. They are the flux field, which is a gradient field, so it has no circulation. Arrows run from each chip to the fan and toward the edges.
  • Raise the limit slider to 69 °C and the no-fan hot region disappears. Lower it to 45 °C and watch the area metric.
